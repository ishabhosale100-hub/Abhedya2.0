ABHEDYA 2.0
Protect. Prove. Prevail.

**An AI-powered digital safety platform designed to help women creators, aspiring actors, and young people navigate online risks with greater awareness and control.**

 About the Project

The internet has created incredible opportunities for people to express themselves, build careers, showcase talent, and connect with the world. But it has also made it easier for someone to misuse another person's photographs, spread harmful comments, or exploit someone who is trying to build a career.

I wanted to work on a problem that goes beyond simply blocking an account or reporting a post. I wanted to explore how technology could help people identify potential risks, preserve evidence, and make more informed decisions before a situation gets worse.

That is how **ABHEDYA 2.0** came to life.

ABHEDYA is a digital safety platform that brings together image protection, photo comparison, harmful-comment analysis, and casting safety into one place.

The idea is simple: people should be able to create, share, audition, and build their careers without having to constantly worry about how their identity, photographs, or ambitions might be misused.

I am building ABHEDYA as a practical, evolving project that combines AI, image processing, machine learning, and accessible web technology to explore these challenges.

 The Problem I'm Trying to Solve

1. Photo Morphing, Image Misuse, and Digital Identity Abuse

A photograph shared online can travel far beyond its original audience. Someone may download it, alter it, combine it with another image, or use it in a misleading or humiliating context without the person's permission.

AI-generated imagery and increasingly accessible image-editing tools make this concern even more serious.

For the person affected, the consequences may include:
- Loss of privacy and control over personal images.
- Emotional distress, embarrassment, and reputational damage.
- Impersonation and misleading representations of their identity.
- Difficulty documenting what changed between two images.
- Uncertainty about how to preserve useful evidence when reporting misuse.

The problem is not that people share photographs. The problem is that they can lose control over how those photographs are reused.

**What I want ABHEDYA to do:** provide tools for image protection, watermarking, comparison, and evidence preservation that help users examine potential misuse and document what they find.

### 2. Harmful Comments, Online Harassment, and Cyberbullying

For many creators, receiving comments is part of building an online presence. Unfortunately, the same comment sections can become places for targeted harassment, degrading remarks, threats, sexual harassment, and repeated unwanted attention.

A single harmful comment can be upsetting, but repeated comments across posts and platforms can make someone feel unsafe in a space where they are trying to work, learn, or express themselves.

This is particularly concerning for younger users who are still developing their confidence and learning how to navigate public online spaces.

Some harmful messages are obvious. Others depend on context, wording, repetition, and the surrounding conversation.

**What I want ABHEDYA to do:** help users identify potentially harmful comments, understand why a message may have been flagged, and make better-informed decisions about documenting or reporting concerning interactions.

 3. Unsafe Casting Practices and Exploitation of Aspiring Actors

For an aspiring actor, creator, or model, an audition can represent an important opportunity. That ambition can also make it difficult to recognize or challenge an unsafe request, especially when someone claims to control access to a role or career opportunity.

Potential warning signs include:
- Requests for registration or selection fees under suspicious circumstances.
- Artificial deadlines and pressure to make immediate decisions.
- Instructions to keep an audition secret or attend an isolated meeting.
- Requests for intimate photographs or inappropriate personal content.
- Pressure to perform uncomfortable acts in exchange for a role.
- Threats, coercion, or promises that depend on complying with personal demands.

Not every unusual audition request is automatically fraudulent, and an automated system cannot establish someone's intentions with certainty. However, identifying specific warning signs can help an aspiring actor pause, assess the situation, and seek trusted advice.

**What I want ABHEDYA to do:** provide a dedicated Casting Shield that helps users examine potentially suspicious messages and recognize warning signs before deciding how to proceed.


 Why Children and Teenagers Need Particular Attention

Children and teenagers may face additional challenges when navigating online platforms, creator communities, and opportunities related to modelling, acting, or content creation.

They may have less experience evaluating professional claims, identifying manipulation, understanding privacy risks, or responding to someone who uses authority and career promises to pressure them.

For example, a young person might receive a message claiming that they have been selected for an important role, followed by a demand for private photographs or a request to keep the interaction secret from their family.

The promise of an opportunity can make it harder to recognize the warning signs.

Other concerns include:
- Personal photographs being copied or misused.
- Cyberbullying affecting confidence and participation online.
- Pressure to share personal information or intimate content.
- Fake casting opportunities and suspicious payment requests.
- Uncertainty about how to preserve evidence or seek help.

I believe digital safety tools should be understandable, accessible, and designed with these vulnerabilities in mind.

ABHEDYA aims to support awareness and early recognition of potential risks. It is not a substitute for trusted adults, professional safeguarding, appropriate reporting channels, or emergency support. It should never place the responsibility for exploitation on the person experiencing it.

**The goal is to help people recognize warning signs, not to judge or blame them.**


 What ABHEDYA 2.0 Offers

ABHEDYA brings several related digital-safety capabilities into one platform.

 1.  Photo Watermarking

Digital photographs can be copied and redistributed without their creator's knowledge. Watermarking can help establish an association between an image and its creator or provide a mechanism for detecting certain forms of modification.

The Photo Watermarking module focuses on image-protection techniques intended to help users protect their work and preserve useful ownership information.

Depending on the implementation, image-protection techniques may include:
- Watermark embedding and verification.
- Image metadata inspection or preservation.
- Additional image-protection techniques where supported.

Watermarking is not a guarantee against every form of removal, editing, or misuse. Its effectiveness depends on the technique used and the transformations applied to the image.

2.  Photo Comparison

Photo Comparison helps users examine two images and identify differences that may warrant closer inspection.

The module uses image-processing techniques to compare an original image with a suspected altered version.

Depending on the implemented analysis, this may include:
- Structural Similarity Index (SSIM).
- Pixel-level difference analysis.
- Difference masks.
- Heatmaps and visual overlays.
- Visual comparison results that help users inspect changed regions.
- Integration with evidence-preservation workflows where supported.

This can be useful when someone suspects that an image has been altered, but a difference score alone cannot establish malicious intent, prove who edited the image, or determine whether an image is a deepfake.

 3.  Comment Safety

Online harassment is not always easy to identify from a single word or sentence. Comment Safety explores how machine learning and text analysis can help identify potentially harmful messages.

The module is designed to analyze text and flag categories of concern supported by its current model and rules.

Its purpose is to help users:
- Recognize potentially harmful comments.
- Identify messages that may need closer review.
- Understand that context matters when interpreting language.
- Make more informed decisions about moderation and reporting.

Automated classification can produce false positives and false negatives. A flagged message should be treated as a signal for review, not an unquestionable judgment about the person who wrote it.

4. Casting Shield

Casting Shield focuses on the risks that can arise when people pursue acting, modelling, and creator opportunities.

Its existing message-analysis functionality is intended to identify warning signs in casting-related messages, including pressure, secrecy, inappropriate requests, and potential coercion, according to the capabilities of the implemented rules and model.

The broader vision is to make it easier for aspiring actors to recognize suspicious demands, understand why a message may be concerning, and preserve relevant information.

#### Experimental Camera Expression Demo

I am also exploring a camera-based expression demonstration within Casting Shield.

The current concept has two expression states:

- 🟢 **Soft Smile / Calm:** A green indicator reading `CALM EXPRESSION DETECTED`.
- 🔴 **Lip Lick:** A red indicator reading `EXPRESSION FLAG — REVIEW REQUIRED`.

The intended demonstration is a local camera preview with start and stop controls. It is not an actual video call.

This is an experimental expression-based interface, not a reliable detector of exploitation or inappropriate intent. Lip-licking can occur naturally, and ordinary facial movements should not be interpreted as evidence of misconduct.

The detection capability depends on the actual model and its demonstrated performance. The interface must not claim that real detection is operational unless the model has been implemented and tested.

Future development may explore more contextual approaches, but the current expression demonstration should remain clearly distinguished from the existing casting-message analysis.

 5.  Evidence Vault

Identifying a potential problem is only one part of responding to it. Preserving relevant information can also be important when someone needs to review an incident or submit a report.

The Evidence Vault is intended to support the preservation and organization of evidence produced through the platform's supported workflows.

Depending on the implemented functionality, this may include analysis results and associated image or report information.

Evidence should be handled carefully because it may contain personal or sensitive information. Any generated report is a record of the available information and analysis; it is not, by itself, proof of a crime or a guarantee of legal admissibility.

 6.  Creator Safety Score

The Creator Safety Score provides a dashboard-oriented way to present a user's safety-related information and indicators supported by the existing implementation.

Its purpose is to make relevant information easier to understand rather than overwhelm users with disconnected results.

Any score or indicator should be interpreted according to its underlying methodology. It should not be treated as a guarantee that an account, image, interaction, or opportunity is safe.

 How the Technology Fits Together

ABHEDYA combines different technical approaches because image misuse, harmful comments, and unsafe casting requests are different problems.

| Area | Technical approach |
|---|---|
| Photo Watermarking | Image-protection techniques |
| Photo Comparison | Computer vision and image similarity/difference analysis |
| Comment Safety | Text analysis and machine learning |
| Casting Shield | Casting-message analysis using the implemented rules and ML model |
| Camera Expression Demo | Experimental camera and expression-classification workflow |
| Evidence Vault | Evidence and analysis-result organization |
| Creator Safety Score | Presentation of supported safety indicators |

The platform is not based on the assumption that one AI model can solve every digital-safety problem. Each module needs an approach appropriate to the type of information it processes.

 Getting Started

Prerequisites

Install the following before running the project:

- Python
- Node.js and npm
- Git, if you are cloning the repository

 1. Clone the Repository

```bash
git clone <YOUR_GITHUB_REPOSITORY_URL>
cd Abhedya2.0
```

Replace the placeholder with the actual repository URL.

 2. Set Up the Backend

Open a terminal in the project root and run:

```bash
cd backend
python -m venv .venv
```

Activate the virtual environment.

**Windows PowerShell:**

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1
```

Install the backend dependencies:

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
```

Start the backend server:

```bash
python -m uvicorn main:app --host 127.0.0.1 --port 8000
```

If the project exposes FastAPI's interactive documentation, it can typically be accessed at:

`http://127.0.0.1:8000/docs`

 3. Set Up the Frontend

Open a second terminal:

```bash
cd frontend
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the local URL displayed by Vite in your terminal, commonly:

`http://localhost:5173`

Keep the backend terminal running while testing features that depend on the API.

**Note:** These instructions assume the current project uses the stated commands and default local ports. Any environment variables or additional setup required by the actual project must also be configured.

 Privacy and Responsible Use

Digital-safety software must be careful not to create new privacy risks while trying to address existing ones.

ABHEDYA's design principles include:

- Handle images, messages, and evidence carefully.
- Avoid unnecessary collection or storage of personal information.
- Make camera activity visible and require explicit camera permission.
- Do not record or upload camera footage unless a future feature explicitly requires it, has appropriate safeguards, and is transparently disclosed.
- Treat automated results as indicators that may require human review.
- Avoid presenting a model prediction as proof of wrongdoing.
- Recognize that ordinary expressions and behavior vary between individuals.
- Keep limitations visible instead of promising perfect detection.

The experimental camera feature should not be used to determine whether someone is trustworthy, whether an audition is safe, or whether misconduct has occurred. Facial-expression analysis cannot reliably establish those facts.

ABHEDYA is intended to support informed decisions, not replace human judgment or established reporting and safeguarding processes.

Future Scope

ABHEDYA is an evolving project. Some ideas I would like to explore as the platform develops include:

- Improved robustness of image-protection and image-comparison techniques.
- Better evaluation of comment-classification models across varied language and context.
- More comprehensive contextual analysis of suspicious casting requests.
- Carefully evaluated approaches to combining multiple safety indicators.
- Improvements to evidence organization and reporting workflows.
- Additional privacy and security safeguards.
- More accessible and understandable explanations for flagged results.
- Evaluation with diverse, appropriately sourced datasets and clearly defined performance metrics.

These are potential future developments, not claims that the corresponding capabilities already exist.

Any future camera-related work should prioritize a clearly defined task, local processing where feasible, explicit user consent, and testing for false positives and false negatives.

#Limitations

ABHEDYA is a developing project and should be evaluated according to its actual implementation.

- Image-comparison results do not independently prove image manipulation or malicious intent.
- Watermarking does not guarantee that an image cannot be copied or altered.
- Text classifiers may misinterpret sarcasm, context, slang, or regional language.
- Casting-risk indicators cannot establish fraud or exploitation with certainty.
- Facial expressions alone cannot reliably determine intent, consent, or misconduct.
- The camera expression demonstration is experimental and must not be represented as a validated exploitation-detection system.
- The platform does not replace professional advice, emergency assistance, or official reporting channels.

I want the project to grow through testing, feedback, and evidence-based improvements rather than exaggerated claims about AI.

 Why I Built ABHEDYA

I wanted to work on a problem that affects people beyond the technical side of an application.

For me, digital safety is not only about detecting a manipulated image or classifying a harmful sentence. It is also about giving people better tools to understand what might be happening, preserve information, and make decisions with more confidence.

Someone should not have to give up their online presence because they fear image misuse. An aspiring actor should not have to ignore their discomfort because they are afraid of losing an opportunity. A young creator should not have to navigate harmful online interactions without accessible tools or support.

ABHEDYA is my attempt to bring several parts of this problem into one practical platform.

It is not a claim that technology can eliminate exploitation. It is a project about exploring how thoughtful engineering can contribute to safer digital experiences.
